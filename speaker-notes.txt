SLIDE 4: Measurable colour classes
Status: DEFINITION: ORDINARY AREA
Uses: the usual area measure
Source: Walkthrough §5

Say: Here A is a bounded part of one colour class. Measurable means that it
can be squeezed between a compact inner approximation and an open outer
approximation with arbitrarily small area gap. For unbounded classes, apply
this inside every bounded square. Every set has an outer area; matching inner
approximation is essential. The epsilon controls area error, not the radius of
a uniform neighbourhood. These are area statements, not smoothness
assumptions.

SLIDE 5: Weakness is a probability-zero condition
Status: DEFINITION: WEAK COLOURING
Uses: measurable colour classes; position and direction sampling
Source: Definition 1.2; Walkthrough §§4–6

Say: A proper colouring excludes every matching unit pair. Weakness allows
exceptional matching pairs, provided they occupy zero measure in this precise
experiment. We cannot choose a uniform point from the infinite plane, so we
use bounded windows. We must build unit distance into the sampling; two
independent random plane points would almost never be a unit apart regardless
of colouring.

SLIDE 41: The closed 60° cone theorem
Status: TEAM RESULT: CC60 — STATEMENT USED HERE
Uses: actual common-centre sets; approach directions; straddle exclusions
Source: Walkthrough §24; ConeObstruction.lean:150; results ledger CC60

Say: This is a team theorem about one local geometric configuration, separate
from the three-centre replacement. We allow any finite number k of labels. For
every set C_i, there must be actual points q_i,n in C_i other than x with
q_i,n tending to x, and the normalized directions (q_i,n-x)/|q_i,n-x| tending
to v_i. The v_i are oriented unit vectors: reversing a direction is not
permitted just to make the cone narrower. The sets can approach x from other
directions too; no tangent or straight ray is assumed. A common cone of width
60 degrees means that some angle phi has representatives alpha_i in [phi-pi/6,
phi+pi/6]. The conclusion counts labels outside the union of the exclusion
lists; it does not claim that every permitted label actually appears. The
generic theorem does not require the sets to be connected or compact, although
the supplied interfaces have both properties. Its formal hypothesis is that
the spatial essential support of each forbidden label is disjoint from the
corresponding open straddle region. On an open region this is equivalent to
that label having zero area there. Neither a list of arrows nor arbitrary
label pairs supplies these hypotheses. The theorem includes equality at 60
degrees. We are stating its result and role here, not presenting its complete
proof. Source:
HadwigerNelson/Main/ResearchSnapshot/local/outputs/cone/ConeObstruction.lean,
closed60_exclusions_three_le at line 150; binary contradiction form at line
133. Recorded Lean 4.34.1 checks and standard-only axiom audits are in the
cone REPORT.txt / VALIDATION.json and RESULTS-LEDGER.md, CC60. No fresh build
is claimed.

SLIDE 42: What the cone theorem rules out
Status: ILLUSTRATION: AN IMPOSSIBLE LOCAL CONFIGURATION
Uses: CC60; six labels; two exclusion sets 40° apart
Source: Walkthrough §§23–25; cone packet and independent referee

Say: For this illustration, allow six labels. The two actual sets through x
have selected directions 40 degrees apart. One excludes labels 1 and 2 from
its straddle region, the other excludes 3 and 4. In the overlap, only labels 5
and 6 can remain, up to zero-area exceptions. But the approach directions fit
inside a closed 60-degree cone, so CC60 requires at least three labels outside
the two exclusion lists. This is a contradiction. The drawing is an impossible
hypothetical configuration, not a valid six-colouring. Blue and green shading
mark exclusion regions and red marks their overlap; those shadings do not
assign actual colours to plane points. The short straight segments are a
geometric model, with their lengths exaggerated. The proof mechanism is that
directions within a closed 60-degree cone give common OUTER strips along an
open 120-degree arc of the unit circle around x. The intersection is
restricted to one fixed pair of labels if at most two survive. The team proves
that such an outer-strip region cannot be almost everywhere two-coloured
(OA120), and CC60 follows. Widths are locally positive but need not stay
uniformly positive up to the open arc endpoints. The picture also displays
inner overlap, but the CC60 proof uses the common outer side; do not mix inner
and outer sides independently for different interfaces. Usefulness: this
rejects candidate local arrangements once their geometric hypotheses have been
proved, and can strengthen finite searches that faithfully encode those
hypotheses. It does not show that every hypothetical six-colouring must
contain this arrangement, so it does not prove a global no-six result or
improve the supplied bound. If the two excluded pairs instead were {1,2} and
{1,3}, the surviving labels would be {4,5,6}; CC60 alone would give no
contradiction. Sources: ConeObstruction.lean, outer120_binary_impossible:51,
closed60_continua_outer_strips:101, closed60_exclusions_not_binary:133; ASTRA-
CONE-REFEREE.txt §§1–3. The existing PNG was visually checked for its labels,
40-degree setup, six-label universe, and explicit impossible-configuration
caption.
